## What this project does

EV battery packs generate significant heat during driving and fast charging — and thermal mismanagement is a leading cause of degradation and safety incidents. This project builds a full MATLAB simulation of the Tesla Model 3's battery thermal system, quantifying heat generation, modelling the thermal resistance path, and validating that the cooling design keeps cells within safe limits across all operating modes.

The work covers:

- Computing heat generation under 5 real-world scenarios using both I²R and efficiency-based methods
- Building a 4-component thermal resistance network (cell → TIM → cooling plate → coolant)
- Calculating cell surface and core temperatures per scenario
- Modelling coolant temperature rise at a fixed flow rate
- Validating the cooling system against a 45°C safety limit at 250 kW peak fast charging
- Generating publication-ready figures comparing all scenarios

## Thermal Resistance Network

The model uses a series resistance path from cell core to coolant:

```
[Cell Core] → R_cell → R_TIM → R_plate → R_conv → [Coolant]
```

| Component | Resistance (K/W) | Share of Total |
|---|---|---|
| Cell radial conduction | 0.000027 | 0.4% |
| Thermal Interface Material (TIM) | 0.001667 | 19.5% |
| Aluminium cooling plate | 0.000185 | 2.2% |
| Forced convection | 0.000667 | **77.9%** |

**Key finding:** Convective resistance is the dominant thermal bottleneck — improving coolant flow or channel geometry has the highest impact on system performance.

## Key Results

- **Worst-case cell temperature:** 27.4°C at 250 kW fast charging — **17.6°C below the 45°C safety limit**
- **Design validated** with a 20% margin, sized at 24 kW cooling capacity
- **Kurtosis** is the strongest thermal predictor for impulsive fault events
- Coolant temperature rise at peak charging: ~5.7°C over inlet at 10 L/min flow
